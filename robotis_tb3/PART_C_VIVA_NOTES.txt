PART C VIVA PRACTICE - explain these in your own words

1. What are you estimating?
World x and y in metres and base heading in radians, with a 3x3 covariance.
Wheel increments predict movement. Marker range, bearing and orientation-derived
heading correct drift. The controller uses this posterior estimate.

2. Why EKF rather than an ordinary linear Kalman filter?
The circular-arc motion and landmark range/bearing functions are nonlinear.
The EKF propagates their local Jacobians F,G,H at the current estimate.
This approximation can fail with large errors or poor sensor geometry.

3. Where does Q come from?
The baseline combines wheel-increment uncertainty passed through G with a
per-update process floor for unmodelled slip. The values were engineering
choices motivated by observed motion-model errors, not measured specifications.
The grid study tested covariance scales 0.1,1,10. Q x10 improved replay error
and coverage, but its low NEES shows conservative uncertainty.

4. What does multiplying Q by 10 mean?
Variance increases tenfold; standard deviation increases by sqrt(10).
It permits more uncertainty growth and generally stronger camera correction.
During camera loss it also gives large uncertainty. Do not call this perfect
calibration or proof that the true noise is ten times larger.

5. Where does R come from?
Baseline standard deviations are 3 cm range, 1.5 degrees bearing and 8 degrees
heading. These are tuning assumptions for a simulated camera. A real sensor
needs calibrated intrinsics/extrinsics and measured, possibly range-dependent
and correlated residual covariance. All three quantities share image corners.

6. What if R is ten times smaller?
The filter generally trusts the camera more. On reserved replay traces at
Q x1, reducing R to x0.1 gave about 6.01 cm RMSE versus 6.18 cm baseline, but
3-sigma coverage fell from 95.7% to 90.1% and mean NEES rose from 5.76 to 9.39.
That is an uncertainty trade-off, not a blanket improvement.

7. What happens when the marker disappears?
Skip camera correction; continue encoder prediction and grow P. The position
estimate may drift. On return, wrap angular innovations and gate bad updates.
During docking, translation waits for accepted camera information; the
controller may rotate toward the EKF-predicted landmark bearing to reacquire it.

8. Can one marker observe all three states?
Range and bearing alone to one landmark have a rotational ambiguity about it.
Here the marker orientation is known, and PnP also yields an absolute heading
reference. The combined measurement Jacobian has rank 3 away from the marker.
If heading extraction/calibration fails, that full-pose claim does not hold.

9. Why wrap angles?
A measurement near -pi and prediction near +pi are close physically. Without
wrapping, the residual incorrectly looks almost 2*pi. Wrap predicted state,
angle residuals and angular finite-difference errors.

10. Why the Joseph covariance update?
It helps preserve positive-semidefinite covariance under finite precision.
The implementation also solves S linear systems rather than explicitly
inverting S, and symmetrises P. None of these fixes a wrong noise model.

11. What do NEES, NIS and coverage mean?
NEES=e^T P^-1 e uses truth for offline evaluation. NIS=nu^T S^-1 nu uses the
innovation and can be calculated without truth. For a correctly modelled
three-state Gaussian filter, expected NEES is 3. Our candidate's mean near
0.98 indicates conservative uncertainty. Component-wise +/-3-sigma coverage
is not a joint confidence region. Correlated time samples are not independent
Monte Carlo trials. A good average alone does not prove consistency.

12. What exactly did the study compare?
Nine Q/R settings replayed eight fixed sensor streams: 72 estimator replays.
Two traces were used for the selection screen and six reserved for comparison.
The replay first reproduced the recorded baseline to numerical precision.
The candidate reduced reserved-trace mean position RMSE from 6.18 to 5.04 cm.
It was then checked online in two fresh seed-2 navigation trials. Do not call
all 72 replays new navigation runs, or claim 72/72 docking success.

13. Was the chosen setting optimal?
No. Q x10,R x0.1 had lower replay error but weaker coverage. Our conservative
screen chose Q x10,R x1 for coverage and error. The rule does not penalise
very low NEES, so over-conservatism is an explicit limitation. Default running
parameters remain Q x1,R x1; candidate runs use explicit flags.

14. What failed and how was it diagnosed?
Very low process uncertainty allowed encoder turning-slip error to grow while
valid camera readings were gated out. Another timeout came from docking logic:
a stopped-position latch prevented movement after EKF corrections moved the
estimate outside tolerance. The fix added capture/release hysteresis and bounded
camera-gap handling. This shows estimator-controller interfaces matter too.

15. Where is ground truth used?
Only in physical sensor simulation and named evaluation/logging functions.
PoseEKF and the tracker accept no MuJoCo state. Reconstructed replay wheel
increments come from encoder-only odometry, not true position. The truth is
read for scoring after the filter has produced its estimates.

16. What would you improve next?
Measure camera residual covariance with Gardner, including correlations and
distance/angle dependence. Make process noise depend on measured motion/slip.
Test more independent starts/seeds, systematic bias and longer occlusions.
Coordinate with Wong on stale-measurement handling and uncertainty-aware
control. Review the large yaw covariance during occlusion before any deployment.
